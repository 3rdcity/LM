Five small habits that take the edge off a racing mind

You don't need an hour of meditation. A few steady routines, done most days, can make anxious mornings feel more manageable.

[Author name][Role, e.g. Counselling psychologist] · [Date] · [0] min read

Anxiety rarely arrives all at once. It builds in the background: a tight chest on the commute, a to-do list that keeps rewriting itself at 2am. Big changes help, but small, repeatable habits are often what make the everyday feel lighter.

None of these replace proper support. They're simple things you can start this week, and they work best when you do them on calm days too, not only when things feel bad.

1. Breathe out for longer than you breathe in

When your mind races, your breathing usually speeds up with it. Slowing the out-breath is one of the quickest ways to signal to your body that you're safe. Try breathing in for a count of four and out for a count of six, for a minute or two.

2. Give your worries somewhere to go

Set aside ten minutes in the early evening to write down everything on your mind. When a worry shows up at another time, remind yourself it has a slot later. It sounds too simple. It works more often than you'd think.

3. Move, even a little

A short walk, some stretching, dancing in the kitchen. Moving your body burns off some of the restless energy anxiety leaves behind. Pick something you'll actually do on a busy day.

"You don't have to fix the whole day. You just have to get through the next ten minutes a little more gently."

[Author name], [Role]

4. Protect the last hour before bed

Screens, late coffee and work email all keep your mind switched on. Try a simple wind-down: dim lights, phone in another room, something slow to read. Sleep and anxiety feed each other, in both directions.

5. Name it to tame it

Saying "this is anxiety" out loud, or writing it down, creates a little distance between you and the feeling. You're noticing it rather than being swept along by it.

When habits aren't enough
